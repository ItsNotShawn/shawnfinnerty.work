## The idea

Stand Up is a concept I developed while thinking about how difficult it has become for younger people to engage with political issues without immediately being pulled into argument, noise, or partisan framing. I wanted to explore whether an app could create a calmer space where users contribute one structured response at a time, see broader patterns in what others are concerned about, and better understand the systems behind those issues without the experience feeling like social media.

## Where it started

Political participation often pushes younger users into either passive scrolling or highly reactive discussion spaces. I wanted to explore whether a product could create a lower-pressure alternative, where participation happens through small, structured inputs instead of open debate. Early thinking focused less on features and more on what had to be avoided: public argument loops, partisan framing, and trust barriers caused by identity-heavy onboarding.

From there, I mapped the app around four questions: what should users do each day, what makes collective input feel visible, how should explanation be presented without bias, and what transparency would be necessary if responses were meant to leave the platform as reports. That thinking became the basis for the Today, Pulse, Learn, and Transparency sections.

A large part of the project began as written product exploration before screen refinement. I used documentation to define the product philosophy, feature boundaries, trust requirements, and how civic responses would move beyond the app itself.

## Today

For the Today page, I focused on making participation feel small and approachable, with one structured daily question designed to collect opinion without pushing users into open-ended debate.

## Pulse

Pulse was designed as the collective layer, where I wanted responses to feel visible through trend data and participation patterns rather than comments or social interaction. This mockup is a work in progress.

## Learn

The Learn section came from the idea that participation works better when people can quickly understand what a policy or issue actually means without partisan framing.

## Transparency

I treated transparency as a core trust feature, showing clearly how responses stay anonymous and where aggregated reports would go outside the app.

## Credibility

If anonymous civic input is going to matter, the product needs visible safeguards against manipulation without creating barriers for normal users.
