Mixes are estimates from a pigment-mixing model. Brands vary, so mix a small amount first and adjust. Parts are by volume. When one number is much bigger than the others, start with that paint and add the rest a tiny bit at a time.

How to paint from your blob map
  1. Transfer the shapes. Use the grid to draw the blob edges onto your canvas, one square at a time.
  2. Block in, back to front. The Paint order tab works out the order for you. Fill every blob with flat, thin paint. Start with what’s farthest away and work forward. Carry each background color a little past the edges of whatever sits in front of it.
  3. Within each layer, go dark to light. A layer is a depth, like sky, middle ground or foreground, not a grid square. Inside a layer, lay down its darker blobs first, then the lighter ones next to and over them. Depth wins over value: a dark object in the middle ground still waits until the sky behind it is done. Darks first give you the shape and contrast to judge everything else, and the lightest colors stay clean when they go on last. Dark acrylic covers light easily, but light over dark often needs two coats.
  4. Cover the whole canvas before adding detail. It will look rough at this stage. That’s normal.
  5. Refine. Soften edges where shapes melt together. Add mid-sized shapes and color shifts inside the big blobs.
  6. Finish with the extremes. Brightest highlights, deepest accents, texture and fine lines go on last, because nothing goes on top of them.
What if the farthest thing is also the lightest?

Paint it first anyway. “Back to front” decides what overlaps what. “Dark to light” decides the order of paint within an area. A bright sky still goes down first. Paint it a step darker than its brightest spots, then add the brightest glow at the very end.

Should I dry brush?

Yes, but only at the end, over paint that’s already dry. Don’t use it for blocking in. Blocking in needs flat, smooth coverage.

Dry brushing gives broken, sparkly texture, which suits spray, foam, grass, bark, weathered wood, rock and light glinting on water. Load a stiff brush with a little paint and wipe most of it off on a paper towel. Then drag it lightly with the side of the brush so it only catches the raised texture.

For soft hazy effects like mist, glow or a light veil over a sky, scumble instead. Scrub a thin, semi-opaque layer lightly over the area.

Acrylic tips

Acrylic dries fast and dries slightly darker. Mix a little lighter than your target. Blend edges while they’re still wet. Mist your palette with water, or use a stay-wet palette, so your mixes last. Two thin coats beat one thick one.
